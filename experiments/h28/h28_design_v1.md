## 1. Hypothesis

With the third odour D as H27 registered it (value 0 in the agent's read-out, never reinforced, background whiffs at p_D 0.03 anywhere), the adopted agent composed with three channels and given a burst-ranked value tie (Agent16: when present non-negative odours tie at the top value, only the one that most recently delivered three whiffs within 10 steps may steer or keep) (i) keeps its choice in the H21 task within an allowed gap of itself without D on the same rows; and (ii) in the absent-odour world W1 with D, loses at least 20 points fewer rows than the same agent without the rule (H27's Agent15). With D absent the rule never acts, so it is the adopted agent.

Stated narrowly: a statement about supplied values +1/0/0, G 2, C0, the adopted modules (gain, gate, filter with the presence counter, the N2 release), learning off, H27's D at p_D 0.03, these worlds. It is a statement about whether the recognition core can rank two equal-valued odours by a statistic of their own whiff streams, not about value, learning, or any fly.

With V absent, D is in the top set as the tie {B, D} (W1D 0.366 of (step, row)) or alone {D} (0.526) (diag:105), and its whiffs steer on two paths: with nothing held (W1D 3469/3469 D-whiff steps; T1D 764/764) and with D held through `keep` (W1D 2922/2922; T1D 585/585) (diag:71, :61). **Measured.**

### 2.2 The window the change has to act in (inferred from measured numbers)

- **T1D.** In the prior-expiry rows V becomes absent at step 59 (the prior, ph28.py:97, start 240, window 300). Without D the next V whiff comes 54/56/59 steps later (quartiles; diag:188); with D the first D event comes 13/27/39 steps after step 59 (diag:188). So a rule has about 56 steps, starting at step 59, in which D must not steer. D delivers 0.03 x 56 = 1.68 whiffs there (expected), and 0.03 x 59 = 1.77 before it.
- **W1D.** Nav cannot occur before step 59 (V present by its prior, never whiffing; H27 reading R8, and the first D-on/D-off difference at step 59 or later, diag:211). Every row passes more than 3 upwind of the sources at steps 42/44/45/46/52 (diag:164); without D, 383 of 400 come back downwind (diag:167) and the rows reached on the return arrive at B at about step 108-112 (first arrival quartiles 29/30/108/112, diag:205). So the D-free stretch that decides the first return is about steps 59-110; D delivers about 1.5 whiffs in it.
- **Consequence.** Any rule that needs several D events to recognise D (a rate estimate, a surge-contingency estimate) has at most two or three D whiffs to work with before the capture begins (the first D-driven nav at steps 59/72/88, diag:69).

### 2.3 What separates D from a plume in the world's own statistics (read, then arithmetic)

- **The plume.** Inside the cone the per-step whiff probability is 0.3 exp(-d/12), d the along-wind distance from the source; within 3.0 of the source it is 0.3 exp(-max(d, 0)/12); outside both it is 0 (ph11.py:78-83; LAM 12, LMAX 25, W0 1.5, SLOPE 0.25 at ph9.py:34). Values: d 25 (the cone's end) 0.0374; d 20 (the task start) 0.0567; d 15 0.0860; d 10 0.1304; d 5 0.1978; at the source 0.30. **Read, arithmetic.**
- **D.** 0.03 per step everywhere, independent of position: measured 7187/240000 = 0.02995 [0.02927, 0.03064], inside the cones 0.03069, outside 0.02984 (bench:40). About 18 D whiffs per row per 600 steps. **Measured.**
- **Instantaneous rate (short windows).** Wherever the plume can be sensed its rate exceeds D's, by 1.25 at the cone's end, 1.89 at the task start and 10 at the source. A rule that discounts the odour sensed MORE (habituation in its usual sense) removes the plume odour before D at every in-cone position. **Inferred.**
- **Long-run rate (long windows).** For a moving agent the plume is often out of reach: W1 without D, B delivered 2609 + 956 = 3565 whiffs over steps 59-599 in 400 rows (every B whiff steered with V absent; diag:73, :77), 8.9 per row, 0.0165 per step; T1 without D, V delivered 4094 whiffs over 600 steps (all V whiffs steer while V is present; diag:63), 10.2 per row, 0.0171 per step. D's 0.030 is higher. **Measured.** Over long windows D is the MORE frequent odour, so a long-window habituation would have the right sign on average. It fails on timing and on excursions: a trace with a window long enough not to drop V during tracking silences (the valued plume's silences reach 150-200 steps: the N sweep's T1 need, N >= 200, record:h24-run2-n-sweep-result; the longest any-odour silence per T1 row 154/160/171, H17 bench:39) needs about that many steps to rise from its start, so at steps 59-115 D's trace is about 0.03 (1 - e^(-115/300)) = 0.0096 and D is not discounted when the capture begins (2.2); and a source dwell (W1 dwell 24.75 steps within 3.0 of B per row, diag:205, at about 0.27 per step, about 6.7 whiffs) lifts B's trace by about 6.7/300 = 0.022, above D's, exactly where B's whiffs must steer. **Inferred.**
- **Clustering.** What does separate them at every timescale is that a plume sensed near its source arrives in bursts and D does not. Define a burst of an odour as three of its whiffs within 10 consecutive steps. D: at each D whiff, P(at least two more D whiffs in the previous 9 steps) = 1 - 0.97^9 - 9 x 0.03 x 0.97^8 = 1 - 0.76023 - 0.21161 = **0.0282**; D bursts arrive at 0.03 x 0.0282 = 0.000845 per step, 0.51 per 600 steps (P(at least one) 0.40), 0.047 in a 56-step window (P 0.046). The plume near its source (p about 0.27): P(at least 3 in 10 steps) = 1 - (0.73^10 + 10 x 0.27 x 0.73^9 + 45 x 0.27^2 x 0.73^8) = 1 - (0.0430 + 0.1590 + 0.2645) = **0.53**; over a dwell of 8 steps 0.37, of 7 steps 0.29. At the task start (0.0567): 0.016 per 10 steps, against D's 0.0028 per 10 steps. So a burst marks an odour sensed near its source; D produces one rarely and at a known rate. **Inferred** (Bernoulli arithmetic on read and measured rates).
- **The H17 silence table and the N sweep** say the same from the other side: the tracked plume's stream has long gaps (T1 longest any-odour silence per row 154/160/171, p90 193, H17 bench:39; a uniform stream at the task-start rate 0.057 has 33/39/46, H17 bench:41), and no fixed presence window meets both T1 (N >= 200) and W1 (N <= 120) (record:h24-run2-n-sweep-result). A presence or rate rule tuned on gaps cannot tell a sparse uniform stream from a plume the agent is tracking intermittently. **Measured, inferred.**

### 2.4 What that implies for W1D (inferred from measured counts)

- In 97 of 400 W1D rows B delivered no whiff at all in the D-on run (diag:199: last B whiff 'rows with none 97'). W1 trajectories with and without D are identical before step 59 (first differing step 59, diag:211; nav cannot occur before 59), so these rows have no B whiff by step 58 in either run.
- In those rows B is absent by its counter from step 59 (never sensed; the prior ends, ph28.py:97, ph32.py:217) and V is absent, so D is the ONLY present non-negative odour whenever it is present: top = {D}, not a tie (ph32.py:187-188). No statistic of the odours' own streams can rank D below an odour never sensed, and a rule that stops a lone odour from steering until it has shown a burst would also stop B in W1 without D (3.1 (ii')), which changes the adopted agent.
- So under any candidate in section 3 that leaves the adopted agent unchanged without D, W1D keeps at least about 97 lost rows (bench seeds; the count is re-measured at (w), section 4). H27's W1D bars against D off (lost-row DP <= +0.05, dwell >= -0.20 x D_off) then fail with probability near 1: lost-row DP >= (97 - 22)/400 = +0.19. W1D is therefore registered here on what a rule can change, the recovery over H27's agent (M5(a')), and H27's bars are reported.

### 2.5 The fly, recalled and not verified

- Recalled, not verified: Drosophila olfactory receptor neurons adapt to sustained odour (the response declines during a maintained stimulus), and prolonged exposure produces a longer-lasting habituation in the antennal lobe attributed to local-neuron plasticity; both reduce the response to the odour that is present more. This is the grounding of candidates (i) and (iii) below, and 2.3 shows they have the wrong sign at the whiff scale here.
- Recalled, not verified: walking flies have been reported to base upwind and turning decisions on the timing or frequency of odour encounters. That is closer to the recommended candidate's use of bursts. No finding is cited as support; the recommended rule has no fly grounding claimed.
- The model's D is a stream of discrete whiffs at 0.03 per step, the same form as a plume's far-field whiffs. A real ubiquitous odour would more likely be a continuous background concentration, which an adaptation mechanism would remove easily; that is a different condition (section 11, section 12 point 2).
