## 1. What was asked

## 5. Reading (interpretation, not measured)

- **The twin identity holds exactly (F1).** Any rule on the agent's own state acts identically in a T3b row and its T1 twin before the twin's next valued whiff, as the design argued. F6 confirms it pair by pair (385/385 at L; 20/20 at L and L + 48). What F6 adds is a population difference: T3b losses tend to follow a recent neutral whiff (low neutral counter) more often than the long T1 silences do, near the 0.65 vs 0.35 line and far from the registered 0.80 vs 0.20. Per pair it cannot act differently, so it could only choose which rows are opened early in both worlds. Whether that pays is not measured here.
- **The window is not the only lever; its alignment with the cast loop decides the outcome (F2 NOT MET, the (t) profile).** The blind steps follow the cast loop exactly (`since` = t - L in every row). At L + 100 every agent is upwind. At L + 200 almost every agent is on the loop's downwind pass through the neutral plume's along range, just outside its width. At L + 300 99 percent are upwind again. A window of 300 opens when the agent is upwind, so it waits for the next pass. Windows of 60 and 150 give the same surge and dwell. A window of 200 opens inside the first pass. A window of 250 already misses it (none 189; dwell 4.115, about the same as 300). The first pass runs from about L + 173 to about L + 242 on these rows. So the design's (e1) works because 200 falls inside that pass, and a window 50 steps longer gets nothing. That is a sensitivity to the cast phase (t0, geometry, cast parameters), which design v1 does not model (its section 11 lists other t0 as untested).
- **'Window opens, then recovery' does not hold at 300 (F3 NOT MET).** Surges come 171/176/180 steps after L + 300, on the second pass. Rows whose second pass falls after 599 never recover. The uncensored subset (0.818) says recovery comes at the next pass when there is time for it, not when the window opens.
- **The twins are a return-loop population (F4 INCONCLUSIVE).** 91.7 percent of the T1 twins whiff the valued odour again within 200 steps, but at a median of 153 steps, not under 100. The twin meets the valued plume on the same first pass (151/153/156) on which the T3b row, with the valued column masked, would meet the neutral plume (173 onward). So the rows at risk in T1 from a shorter window are the rows whose first pass meets the neutral plume before the valued one. With the design's window of 200 that is later than the twins' median return. This is why the T1 cost at 200 can be small. It does not show that the sweep's harmful rows are the same rows.
- **On these seeds the design's candidate costs nothing in T1 and gains more than predicted in T3b (F5 MET).** The DP is +0.0000 (15 rows depart, no outcome changes), against the sweep's -0.0125 at N 200 on other seeds. The T3b gain is +2.460 [+1.883, +3.055], above the design's predicted range (+0.5 to +1.3, from the T3a analog). Agent17 exceeds the window-60 arm (R_b 1.35): surging on the first pass later (213 to 242 after L, not 173 to 210) seems to leave the agent at the neutral source in the late measurement window 400-599. That is inferred, not measured. This is one seed set, the one the H26 bench used.
- In short: two assumptions under the design fail as registered (F2, F3), two are unresolved (F4, F6), and the direct T1 and T3b measurements of the design's own candidate are favourable. The favourable result depends on a window that sits inside one pass of the cast loop.
